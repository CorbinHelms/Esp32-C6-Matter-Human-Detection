1 Check what you need

  • A Thread border router on your Wi-Fi: Nest Hub (2nd gen), Nest Hub Max, Nest Wifi Pro, or Google TV Streamer. Without one it can't join.
  • Google Home app, Bluetooth on, and USB-C power near the spot (any phone charger).
  • Your Gmail added to the maker's developer project — see the back cover.

2 Snap the sensor into the mount

  1. One layer of foam tape flat on the pocket floor.
  2. Radar side (white board) faces out; USB-C toward the open slot.
  3. Slide one edge under the wide lip, press the other until it clicks past the tabs.
  4. Remove by lifting the tab edge with a fingernail.

3 Pick the spot

  • Wedge mount: flat wall, ~2.2–2.7 m up, facing the room. It aims the radar 45° down.
  • Corner mount: top corner of the room, near the ceiling.
  • Wide beam (±60°), ~5 m reach walking, less when still — point it at where people actually are.
  • Avoid fans, swaying plants, and thin shared walls (radar sees through them a little).
  • Cable slot faces the floor.

4 Stick it to the wall

  • Wipe the wall (isopropyl is best), let it dry.
  • Foam tape only on the raised strips: the two back rails (wedge) or the pad at each wing end (corner). The rest of the back is recessed on purpose.
  • Press 30 seconds right over the taped areas — corner mount: press the wing ends, not the middle.

5 Power it & pair

  1. Cable out of the bottom slot, into any USB-C charger. A slow-blinking light means it's running; each power-up opens a fresh 15-minute pairing window.
  2. Google Home app: Devices → Add → Matter-enabled device.
  3. Scan the QR on the front of this pamphlet (or type 0109-114-9528).
  4. Accept the "device isn't certified" notice, pick your Thread network, give it a room.
  5. Don't cancel — slow attempts finish late rather than fail. Give it up to 5 minutes.

6 Make it do things

It shows up as an occupancy sensor. In Google Home automations use the starter “Occupancy → Occupied / Unoccupied”: lights on when someone walks in, off a little after the room empties. It trips within a second or two and clears several seconds after the room is truly empty — no flickering while you sit still. That's the whole point.
